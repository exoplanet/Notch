# How to Find Your Tinnitus Frequency and Set a Notch for Music

This is a listening guide for shaping music. It is not a hearing test, a diagnosis, or a treatment.

## Safety first

- Work in a quiet room and use headphones.
- Keep the volume low. The test tone only needs to be loud enough to hear.
- Do not try to mask or drown out tinnitus with loud sounds.
- Stop if anything feels uncomfortable. Rest, and try again another day at a lower volume.

## Finding your tinnitus frequency

Use a pure-tone generator or a dedicated tinnitus frequency matcher. The free tool below is one option.

1. Start with a broad sweep. Play about 1,000 Hz, then 4,000 Hz, then 8,000 Hz. After each tone, note whether your tinnitus sounds higher or lower.
2. Narrow the range. If the tinnitus sits between two of those tones, try the frequencies in between. Move in smaller steps until an external tone matches the tinnitus or blends with it.
3. Check for an octave error. A tone an octave away can feel like the same pitch. Play half your candidate frequency, then double it. If one of those blends more cleanly, use that frequency instead.
4. If the two ears differ, match each ear on its own.
5. Repeat the match on a different day. Small differences are normal. An approximate match is usually enough.

Most people’s tinnitus falls between about 3,000 and 8,000 Hz. It can sit outside that range. In Notch, a center can be set from 500 Hz to 10,000 Hz.

## Notch width for music

Start with a width of ¼ to ½ octave, centered on the matched frequency. In Notch the Width control is already in octaves:

- ½ octave is **0.5**. This is the width to start with. It is more forgiving, and it is the width often used in research.
- ¼ octave is **0.25**. On the 0.1 step control, **0.2** or **0.3** is the nearest setting. A narrower notch removes less musical content.

A fractional octave scales with the center frequency, so the same Width applies to every notch. A 0.5 setting is about 350 Hz wide at 1,000 Hz and about 1,400 Hz wide at 4,000 Hz. Use that same fractional width for each notch.
